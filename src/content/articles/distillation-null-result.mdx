My current question is whether distillation from a frozen teacher improves a small CKKS-secured federated model when hospitals have skewed data. I fixed the main comparison in advance: **Experiment E** (federated, with distillation) against **Experiment D** (identical, without), paired by fold, on test balanced accuracy. This month a full run finished. The answer, so far, is no.

<Callout type="warn" title="What these numbers are">
This is a preliminary development run on the combined augmented and OASIS images, with three folds and a feature-based distillation design I have since changed. It is not a paper result. I'm writing it up because the way the statistics disagree is worth understanding on its own.
</Callout>

## The primary endpoint

| Fold | D (no distillation) | E (distillation) | E − D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.4374 | 0.4397 | +0.0023 |
| 2 | 0.5666 | 0.5998 | +0.0332 |
| 3 | 0.5702 | 0.5387 | −0.0315 |
| **Mean** | | | **+0.0013** |

- E is higher in 2 of 3 folds
- paired bootstrap 95% interval over test images: **[−0.0015, +0.0040]**, which includes zero
- corrected resampled t-test (Nadeau and Bengio): $t = 0.045$, $p = 0.968$, with 2 degrees of freedom

By the rule I set beforehand, this is a null result.

## Three statistics that seem to disagree

**Accuracy went up in every fold.** By 2.5, 5.7 and 3.3 points, a mean of +3.8, with $p = 0.13$.

**Balanced accuracy didn't move.** As above.

**McNemar's test was overwhelming.** In fold 2, E was right where D was wrong on 2,137 images, and the reverse on 642, giving $p \approx 3 \times 10^{-186}$.

All three are correct. They answer different questions.

## Reconciling them

E predicts "non-demented" more often. On a test set where most images are non-demented, that alone raises accuracy. Very mild recall fell from 55% to 31%. Balanced accuracy weights every class equally, so the gains and losses cancel.

McNemar's test asks whether two **specific, fixed models** disagree asymmetrically on **these test images**. With 26,169 test images, almost any systematic difference is "significant", including a difference in which class a model prefers. It says nothing about whether the method generalises to new training runs, which is what the fold-level test measures, with only three folds.

| Statistic | Question it answers | Here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy difference | Does E get more images right? | Yes, by favouring the majority class |
| Balanced accuracy difference | Does E do better across classes? | No |
| McNemar's test | Do these two models differ on this test set? | Yes, trivially, with this many images |
| Corrected t-test over folds | Would the method win again with new training? | No evidence, with 3 folds |

## Distillation did transfer something

On the test set, E's student moved towards the teacher on every similarity measure:

| Measure | D | E |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prediction agreement with teacher | 0.597 | 0.643 |
| Linear CKA with teacher's features | 0.492 | 0.595 |
| Feature distillation loss | 1.027 | 0.679 |

So the student learned to look more like the teacher. It just didn't become a better classifier. Part of the reason is visible in the gap: the centrally trained teacher's balanced accuracy was 0.825, and D's 0.525. Distillation closed 0.4% of that gap.

## A second warning sign

In a later run on the augmented dataset alone, with a soft-label and attention-map design, centrally trained distillation also looked like this. The distilled student matched the teacher more closely, with KL divergence down from 0.033 to 0.020 and attention-map cosine up from about 0.82 to 0.98, but its test balanced accuracy fell from 90.2% to 89.0%.

## What I'm doing about it

1. **Keep the null.** The endpoint was fixed in advance. Changing it now to "accuracy improved" would be exactly the kind of reporting I criticised in my own dissertation.
2. **Separate the two effects.** The teacher was trained on the server's public data, which D never sees. E against D can't distinguish "distillation helps" from "public data helps". A D-plus-public-data baseline is needed.
3. **Protect the test set.** The notebook records that the final distillation settings were fixed after earlier test results had been seen. Any confirmatory run needs a fresh locked split, used once.
4. **More folds.** With three, the t-test has 2 degrees of freedom and very little power.

<Callout type="key">
A null result from a fair test is more useful than a positive result from a test that was adjusted until it passed. This one tells me the simple version of the idea doesn't work under these conditions, and roughly why.
</Callout>
