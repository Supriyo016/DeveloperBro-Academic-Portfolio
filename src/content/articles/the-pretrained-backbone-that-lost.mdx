EfficientNetB0 scores 77.1% on ImageNet, against 72.0% for MobileNetV2. In my dissertation, under the same federated protocol, it reached a macro F1 of 79.2% with FedAvg, against 92.8% for MobileNetV2 and 94.8% for the Custom CNN. Neither FedProx nor FedDyn helped. This post is about why.

## Four suspects

### 1. Batch Normalisation under skewed clients

EfficientNetB0 normalises with batch statistics in every block. Each client computes them on its own skewed data, and the parameter export averages the running statistics across clients. MobileNetV2 has the same exposure, though, and it reached 92.8%. So BatchNorm alone can't explain a 13-point gap between the two pretrained models.

### 2. Resolution far below the design point

EfficientNetB0 was designed and scaled for 224 × 224 input. At 64 × 64, every feature map is 3.5 times smaller on each side, so the network's receptive fields and its squeeze-and-excitation statistics operate on far less spatial detail than they were tuned for. Touvron and colleagues showed that even a modest change between training and test resolution costs accuracy. A change of this size, between pretraining and fine-tuning, plausibly costs more.

### 3. Frozen layers that don't fit the domain

To match the trainable budget, 1,479,532 of EfficientNetB0's 4,055,296 parameters stay frozen at their ImageNet values. Raghu and colleagues found that ImageNet features transfer less to medical images than is often assumed. Freezing a large share of a network that is already being used at the wrong resolution leaves less room to adapt.

### 4. Optimisation budget

The learning curve was still rising at round 30, by about 0.2 points per round. With a learning rate of $10^{-4}$ and 30 rounds, EfficientNetB0 may simply be slower, and a longer run would narrow the gap. It would be very unlikely to close 15 points, though.

## What the evidence supports

| Suspect | Consistent with results? | Ruled out? | What would test it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BatchNorm | Partly | No, but MobileNetV2 limits it | Swap BN for GroupNorm, or keep BN local (FedBN) |
| Resolution | Yes | No | Rerun at 128 and 224 pixels |
| Frozen layers | Yes | No | Unfreeze everything, accepting a larger payload |
| Budget | Partly | No | Run 100 rounds |

The honest answer is that my design can't tell these apart. Architecture, normalisation, pretraining, resolution and freezing all differ between EfficientNetB0 and the Custom CNN at once, and each configuration was run once.

<Callout type="key" title="What I can say">
Under this protocol, small images and skewed clients, ImageNet accuracy was a poor guide to federated performance. The more-tuned pretrained model was the one that transferred worst. That is worth reporting, as long as it is reported as an observation and not as a proof of one cause.
</Callout>
