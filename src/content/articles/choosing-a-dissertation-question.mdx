The dissertation is the main work of the second year of my M.Tech, and the first step is a question. I spent the last few weeks turning a pile of papers into one. This is how that went.

## What the literature looked like

By now I've read a few dozen papers that combine some of: Alzheimer's or other medical imaging, federated learning, and homomorphic encryption. Sorted into a table, a pattern showed up.

| Study type | Model | Privacy | Data split | Typical claim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Centralised Alzheimer's CNNs | Many | None | Pooled | High accuracy |
| Federated Alzheimer's studies | One CNN or transformer | Plain FedAvg | Multi-site or simulated | Close to centralised |
| Encrypted federated imaging | One CNN or U-Net | HE or secure aggregation | Multi-site | Accuracy kept, cost rises |

Each row is a reasonable study. But almost every paper changes several things at once: a different dataset, backbone, image size, client split and encryption setup. So when two papers disagree, there is no way to tell why. I couldn't find a study that held the data and privacy setup fixed and varied the model and the federated algorithm.

## The question

> When data is non-IID across clients, which CNN backbone and which federated algorithm should be preferred, once every other factor is held constant?

It is narrower than "can we do private Alzheimer's detection?", which is the point. A narrow question can be answered by one person in two semesters.

## Two factors, three levels each

| Backbone ↓ / Algorithm → | FedAvg | FedProx | FedDyn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MobileNetV2 | Experiment 1 | Experiment 2 | Experiment 3 |
| EfficientNetB0 | Experiment 4 | Experiment 5 | Experiment 6 |
| Custom CNN | Experiment 7 | Experiment 8 | Experiment 9 |

| Factor | Levels | Why these |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backbone | MobileNetV2, EfficientNetB0, a Custom CNN | Two common pretrained efficient networks, and one designed for federated training |
| Federated algorithm | FedAvg, FedProx, FedDyn | The standard baseline and two well-known corrections for client drift |

A full factorial design also lets me ask whether the factors interact: does the best algorithm depend on the backbone?

## What stays fixed

The comparison is only fair if everything else is identical across all nine runs:

- the dataset and the held-out test set
- the non-IID partition across five clients, with the same seed
- image size, optimiser, learning rate, batch size and local epochs
- the number of communication rounds
- the CKKS encryption parameters
- the number of trainable parameters, as closely as I can match it

The last item needs explaining. Encrypted communication cost grows with the number of parameters, so a backbone with twice the parameters would be twice as expensive to protect. Matching trainable parameters means the backbones differ in architecture, not in budget.

## What I'm worried about already

Compute. Nine federated runs, each with five clients and many rounds, on whatever free GPU time I can get. That budget will decide how many repeats I can afford, and I'll have to be honest about it when I write up the results.
