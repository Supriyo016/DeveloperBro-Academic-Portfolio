"Non-IID" is one of the most used terms in federated learning, and one of the least looked at. Papers tend to report a concentration parameter and move on. When I partitioned the data for my dissertation, I found it more useful to look at what each client actually ended up holding.

## The partition in a few lines

For each class, draw a set of proportions from a Dirichlet distribution and hand out that class's images accordingly. For class $c$ the shares are drawn as $\mathbf{p}_c \sim \mathrm{Dir}(\alpha)$, and a smaller concentration value $\alpha$ pushes each class onto fewer clients.

```python
import numpy as np

def dirichlet_partition(labels, n_clients=5, alpha=0.5, seed=42):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    clients = [[] for _ in range(n_clients)]
    for c in np.unique(labels):
        idx = rng.permutation(np.where(labels == c)[0])
        proportions = rng.dirichlet(alpha * np.ones(n_clients))
        cuts = (np.cumsum(proportions)[:-1] * len(idx)).astype(int)
        for client, part in zip(clients, np.split(idx, cuts)):
            client.extend(part.tolist())
    return clients
```

## What α = 0.5 produced

Here is the training pool across my five simulated clients:

| Client | Mild | Moderate | Non-demented | Very mild | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2,780 | 1,847 | 3,494 | 184 | 8,305 |
| 2 | 183 | 478 | 2,180 | 680 | 3,521 |
| 3 | 166 | 3,398 | 212 | 2,139 | 5,915 |
| 4 | 4,038 | 1,475 | 268 | 477 | 6,258 |
| 5 | 833 | 802 | 4,086 | 5,480 | 11,201 |

Two different things are skewed here.

**Quantity.** Client 5 holds more than three times as many images as Client 2. Weighted averaging gives Client 5 more say in every round.

**Label mix.** Non-demented scans are 61.9% of Client 2 but only 3.6% of Client 3. Client 1 has almost no very mild cases, at 2.2% of its data. Each client trains towards a different problem.

## Why this is worth looking at

A single α hides both effects. Two partitions with the same α can behave differently depending on the random draw, so I would now always print this table before training anything.

It also changes how to read results. In my runs, very mild cases had the lowest F1 score in all nine configurations. That class is both the hardest clinically and, on some clients, nearly absent from local training. I cannot separate those two causes from this experiment alone, but the table is what suggested the question.

## A limit worth stating

This table comes from one seed and one value of α. A sweep over α, with repeated seeds, would say much more about how methods respond to heterogeneity than a single partition can.
