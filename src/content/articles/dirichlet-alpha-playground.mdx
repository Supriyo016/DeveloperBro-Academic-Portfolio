My dissertation needs a non-IID split of the training data across five simulated hospitals. The standard tool is a Dirichlet partition, popularised for federated learning by Hsu, Qi and Brown in 2019. It has one knob, the concentration $\alpha$. Before picking a value, I wanted to see what each value produces.

## How the partition works

For each class $c$, draw a vector of client shares from a symmetric Dirichlet distribution, and deal that class's images out in those proportions:

$$
\mathbf{p}_c = (p_{c,1}, \dots, p_{c,K}) \sim \mathrm{Dir}(\alpha, \dots, \alpha), \qquad \textstyle\sum_k p_{c,k} = 1
$$

Small $\alpha$ concentrates each class on a few clients. Large $\alpha$ spreads each class evenly, which approaches an IID split.

```python
import numpy as np

def dirichlet_partition(counts, clients=5, alpha=0.5, seed=0):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    shares = rng.dirichlet([alpha] * clients, size=len(counts))   # one row per class
    return np.floor(shares * np.asarray(counts)[:, None]).astype(int)
```

## The experiment

I used class counts matching an 80% training split of my dataset: 8,000 mild, 8,000 moderate, 10,240 non-demented and 8,960 very mild images, 35,200 in all. For each $\alpha$, I drew 500 partitions with different seeds and recorded three things:

- **largest-class share**: for each client, the fraction of its data in its most common class, averaged over clients
- **client sizes**: the smallest and largest client, as medians over the 500 draws
- **near-empty cells**: how many of the 20 client-class pairs hold under 5% of that client's data

## What one α = 0.5 draw looks like

Averages hide how uneven a single draw is. Here is one partition at α = 0.5, as the percentage of each class sent to each client:

| Class | Client 1 | Client 2 | Client 3 | Client 4 | Client 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mild | 18.7 | 36.5 | 4.1 | 0.0 | 40.7 |
| Moderate | 15.5 | 10.9 | 69.0 | 4.3 | 0.3 |
| Non-demented | 10.3 | 76.3 | 10.3 | 1.4 | 1.6 |
| Very mild | 0.0 | 0.7 | 43.6 | 35.3 | 20.4 |

Client 4 gets almost no mild, moderate or healthy scans. Client 2 gets three quarters of all healthy scans. That is a plausible picture of hospitals with different referral patterns, and a hard one for training.

## The choice

I'm going with **α = 0.5**, for three reasons.

1. At α = 0.1, about half the client-class pairs are near empty and the smallest client can have only a few hundred images. That tests whether training survives at all, more than which method is better.
2. At α of 10 or more, the split is close to IID, and the algorithms designed for drift have nothing to correct.
3. α = 0.5 is used widely enough that results can be set beside other papers.

Two safeguards go into my partition code: every client gets at least a few images of every class before the Dirichlet draw, and very small shares are clipped up, so no client is left with a class it has literally never seen.

<Callout type="note">
A Dirichlet split over labels models label skew only. Real hospitals also differ in scanners, protocols and patient age, which is feature skew. A simulation with one public dataset can't produce that, and I'll have to say so in the limitations.
</Callout>
